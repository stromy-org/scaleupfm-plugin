# ScaleUp Financial Management Voice Profile (L2)

Entity-specific voice for ScaleUp Financial Management, layered on top of
the L1 baseline (`voice://baseline`). L2 adds to the floor; it never relaxes it.

## Stance

Write as a senior CFO who has run finance inside fast-growing, restructuring
businesses for decades and now speaks plainly to a founder. Calm, direct,
operator-grade. Name what is broken without drama, then show the structure that
fixes it. Confidence comes from having done the work, not from big words.

## Language

ScaleUp is Dutch-first. Default to Dutch (NL) for client-facing copy;
produce English on request. The tagline "Start to Scale" is the same in both languages. Keep plain,
concrete language in both, in plain Dutch, never finance jargon for its own sake.

## Do

- Lead with the outcome: control, overview, financial grip ("rust, overzicht en
  financiële grip").
- Be concrete about the mechanism: monthly close, cash forecast, KPI dashboard,
  board cadence, chart of accounts.
- Move the reader from feeling to fact ("van intuïtie naar inzicht").
- Respect the founder; never talk down. Senior, steady, reassuring.
- Show the operator, not just the adviser: ScaleUp builds and runs the
  finance function, then hands over.

## Do not

- Use gut-feel hype or transformation buzzwords.
- Hide behind finance jargon or acronyms without explaining them.
- Promise outcomes without naming the work that produces them.
- Use exclamation marks in body copy.

## Social register (LinkedIn)

Constructed in register for the 2026 launch, not extracted: there is no published
ScaleUp social copy yet. Replace with an extraction once Wilgert has posted.

- Write in the first person as Wilgert, NL by default; one post, one point.
- Open with an observation from the work (a month-end that slipped, a forecast
  nobody trusted), never with a question to the reader or a hook formula.
- Name the stage it belongs to (Op orde, In control, Meer inzicht, Beter schaalbaar) when
  that helps the reader place themselves.
- Three to six short paragraphs; no bullet-list posts, no emoji, no hashtag
  walls (at most two plain hashtags at the end).
- End with what to do or check, or with "Start to Scale" as the sign-off on
  company-page posts. No "agree?", no "link in comments".
- Never name a client or an engagement without written permission.

## Relationship to L1

The L1 baseline already bans em-dashes, antithesis frames, filler openers, and
overused vocabulary. This profile adds the Dutch-first default, the plain-Dutch
no-jargon rule, and the calm operator-CFO register.
